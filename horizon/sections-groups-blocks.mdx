[Sections and blocks](/getting-started/sections-and-blocks) gave the short version: a page is a stack of
sections, and each section holds blocks. This page goes one level deeper, with a real page from the site.

## Three layers

| Layer | What it is | What you see in the sidebar |
|---|---|---|
| **Section** | A band of the page, from edge to edge. On this site almost every band is Horizon's **Section**, with the Tangent rails and the line on top. | The section's own name, for example **Service hero** or **How it works**. |
| **Group** | A box that arranges the blocks inside it: one under the other, side by side, or as a ruled grid. Groups can hold other groups. | **Group**, with an arrow to open it. |
| **Content blocks** | The things visitors read and click: **Text**, **Image**, **Button**, **Card list**, **FAQ category**, **Quote form** and so on. | The block's type, or the first words of its text. |

Groups do the layout work that columns and inner sections did in Elementor. A group's **Direction** setting
puts its blocks one under the other (**Vertical**) or side by side (**Horizontal**), and its **Tangent grid**
settings draw the lines between them. See [Section layout](/blocks/section-layout#group-settings).

## A real page, top to bottom

Here is the **Reverse engineering** service page (/pages/reverse-engineering, template
**service-reverse-engineering**). The first section is shown in full, the way the sidebar nests it.

**Service hero** (section)

- **Breadcrumbs**: the "Home / Services / page" trail, **Tangent style**: **Strip: own ruled row**
- **Group**: holds the whole hero
  - **Group**: the row. **Direction**: **Horizontal**, **Lines**: **Lines between blocks, stacked on tablet**
    - **Group**: the copy, on the left
      - **Text**: "Reverse engineering" (**Eyebrow, leading mark**)
      - **Text**: the page title (**Heading large**)
      - **Text**: the intro (**Lede**)
      - **Group**: the buttons. **Direction**: **Horizontal**
        - **Button**: "Call us now" (**Primary, neutral**)
        - **Button**: "Get a Quote" (**Secondary**)
    - **Group**: the photo, on the right
      - **Image** (**Media**)
  - **Card list**: the four key figures under the hero. **Layout**: **Stats**
    - **Card**: 0.025mm
    - **Card**: STEP / IGES / XT
    - **Card**: 5 - 7 business days
    - **Card**: On-site or ship

So a button in the hero sits five levels deep: section → group → group → group → group → button. Every level
has a job: the outer group holds the hero together, the row splits it in two, the copy group stacks the texts,
and the button group lines up the two buttons.

The other sections of the page follow the same patterns:

| Section | What's inside |
|---|---|
| **What we do** | A head group (eyebrow, heading, intro and a **Pixel mark**), a **Flow chart** with five **Flow node** blocks, a small heading, then a **Card list** of five project types. |
| **How it works** | A head group, then a **Card list** with **Layout**: **Numbered steps** and five cards. |
| **What gets delivered** | A head group, then a **Card list** of three text cards and three photo cards, with **Text cards over their images** on. |
| **Why Tangent Solutions (with boxes)** | A row group: the head group on one side, a **Card list** of four cards on the other. |
| **Where we work** | A row group: the head group, and a group with a **Text** and a **Card list** of industries. |
| **Trusted by** | A head group, then a group of twelve **Image** blocks with **Tangent style**: **Logo**. |
| **On-site scanning** | A **Card list** with a single card. |
| **Case studies** | A head group, then a **Card list** with no cards: its **Blog posts** setting is **Case studies** and **Number of posts** is 4, so it shows the latest four case studies by itself. |
| **Comparison** | A head group, then a **Card list** of two cards. |
| **FAQ** | A row group: the head group, and a group holding the **FAQ category** block. The questions come from the FAQ entries in the admin. |
| **Quote** | A row group: the copy (heading, intro and **Contact details**) on one side, the **Quote form** on the other. |

<Tip>
Three group shapes come back on every page: the **head group** (eyebrow, heading, intro), the **row group**
(two cells side by side) and the **cell group** inside a row. When you add something, put it in the same kind of
group as its neighbors, and it lines up by itself.
</Tip>

## Static blocks and added blocks

Most blocks on the site were **added** to their section. You can drag them, duplicate them, hide them and remove
them.

A few blocks are **static**: they're part of the section's design and always there. You can change their settings,
but you can't remove them, drag them elsewhere or add a second one. On this site:

- the header's **Logo**, **Menu**, **Phone** and **Quote button** (see [Header](/blocks/header));
- the post card in the blog list (**Blog posts** section): its title, details, image and description parts are
  the same for every post, because one card design is repeated for each post;
- the top of the scanner's product page: the breadcrumbs, the product photos and the product details.

Some added blocks also only fit inside one parent. A **Card** lives only in a **Card list**, and a **Flow node**
only in a **Flow chart**. That's why **Add block** inside a card list offers only **Card**.

## Header and footer groups

The header and the footer aren't part of any page template. Each is a **section group**: a small stack of
sections shared by every page. In the sidebar they sit above and below the page's own sections.

| Group | Sections in it | More |
|---|---|---|
| **Header** | **Announcement bar**, then **Header** (logo, menu, phone, quote button) | [Announcement bar](/blocks/announcement-bar), [Header](/blocks/header) |
| **Footer** | **Footer** (about text, industries, helpful links, contact, social links, copyright), **Utilities** (hidden) and **Mobile sticky CTA** (turned off for now) | [Footer](/blocks/footer), [Mobile call bar](/blocks/sticky-bar) |

A change in the header or footer group shows on every page at once. The footer itself is built like a page
section: groups inside groups, with **Text**, **Menu**, **Logo** and **Social media links** blocks at the bottom.

## Hide section when empty

Some blocks can hide the **whole section** they're in when they have nothing to show. The setting is on the block,
not on the section:

| Block | Setting | The section hides when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Card list** | **Hide section when empty** | the list has no cards to show, for example a case studies row when the blog has no posts. |
| **FAQ category** | **Hide section when empty** | no category is picked, or the category has no questions yet. |
| **Text** | **Hide section when blank** | the text is empty, for example when it comes from a page field that's blank on this page. |
| **Image** | **Hide when empty** | Only for the image itself: with no image, it leaves no empty frame. The section stays. |

Two things to know:

- **It only works on the live site.** In the theme editor the section stays visible, often with a short note
  saying what's missing, so you can still fill it in.
- **A missing section isn't always a mistake.** If a section shows in the editor but not on the site, check
  whether its card list, FAQ category or text is empty. See
  [A section disappeared](/help/troubleshooting#a-section-disappeared).

<Note>
Many sections on the service and location pages have this setting on. Leave it as it is: it keeps the page
tidy when a piece of content is missing.
</Note>

## What you can and can't move

**You can:**

- drag a section up or down among the page's sections;
- drag a block up or down inside its section, and into or out of a group in that section;
- hide any section or added block with the eye icon, and show it again later.

**You can't:**

- move the header or footer into a page, or a page section into the header or footer;
- move, remove or duplicate a static block;
- put a **Card** anywhere but a **Card list** (and the same for other blocks tied to one parent);
- drag a block from one section into another. To reuse a block somewhere else, add a new one there and copy its
  settings.

Shopify also sets a few limits: a page template holds at most 25 sections, and blocks can be nested at most
eight levels deep. The site's pages stay well inside both.

<Warning>
Each page has its own template. Moving or hiding a block on one service page doesn't change the other service
pages. If a change belongs on all of them, make it on each page, or ask your developer to do it in one go.
</Warning>
