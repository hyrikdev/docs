## What it does

A narrow column beside the main content of a page. Whatever you put in it (buttons, a short heading, a list of
recent articles) stays in view while visitors scroll down the long text next to it. It's a container: you add
other blocks inside it, the same way you add blocks to a group.

On a large screen (990 pixels wide and up), the column sits to the right of the content, with a thin line on
its left side from top to bottom. As the visitor scrolls, the blocks inside it stay just under the header. If
the column holds a quote form, it gets a light gray background. On tablets and phones there's no room for a
second column, so the column moves under the content (or above it, or is hidden: see **Below 990px**).

The site uses 3 sticky columns:

- **Every blog article** (template **article**): the column beside the post holds the **Call Now** and
  **Get a Quote** buttons and the **Recent posts** list titled "Recent articles". On phones it comes after the
  post. See [Blog blocks](/blocks/blog-blocks#recent-posts).
- **The shared landing template** (template **landing**, a starting point for new landing pages): a
  "Get started" column beside the page text, with two buttons. It's hidden on tablets and phones. See
  [Landing pages](/page-recipes/landing-page#the-shared-landing-layouts).
- **The CNC landing page** (3D scanning services for CNC): the closing "Get started" row is a sticky column on
  its own, **Centred**, and hidden with its section on tablets and phones.

## Where to find it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the page">
    In the theme editor, open the template, for example **article** for the blog posts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the block">
    In the sidebar, open the **Blog post** section and select **Sticky column**. On the **landing** template
    it's in the **Page** section, inside the **Page** and **Body and sticky column** groups.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To add one, select a section or group and choose **Add block › Sticky column**. Then add blocks inside it.

## Settings

**Below 990px** decides what happens on tablets and phones. **Width** and the padding are the same on every
column of the site (360 pixels wide, 48 pixels of padding on each side): keep them.

{/* settings:begin source=block:ts-sticky level=3 */}

Used 3 times across 3 page layouts and header/footer groups. In **Tangent uses**, a number in brackets is how many of those copies use that value.

| Setting | Values | Default | Tangent uses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sticky offset** | 0–100px | 8px | 8px | Space under the header while the column sticks. The height of a sticky header is added automatically. |
| **Width** | 240–480px | 360px | 360px | — |
| **Below 990px** | After the content, Before the content, Hidden | After the content | Hidden (2), After the content (1) | Where the column goes on tablets and phones. |
| **Hide the whole section when hidden** | On, Off | Off | On (1), Off (1) | Shown when **Below 990px** is Hidden. For a section that holds only this column, such as a closing column under the page: below 990px the section, its top line included, is hidden with it. |
| **Centred** | On, Off | Off | Off (2), On (1) | For a column alone in its row: from 990px no side line and its blocks centred. Centre the row as well. |
| **Gap** | 0–100px | 8px | 16px (2), 12px (1) | Space between the blocks inside the column. |

## Data and metafields

None. The column shows the blocks you put in it.

## Limitations

- In the **Page** and **Blog post** sections, blocks can only be stacked one under the other. There, the sticky
  column places itself beside the block just before it, for example the post's text. Keep it right after that
  block.
- The column only sticks on large screens (990 pixels and up). Check the page on a phone after a change.
- To change the buttons in the article column, open the **article** template: the change applies to every
  blog post at once.
